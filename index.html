Academic Record

Prerequisite Coursework

BACHELOR PARTY STUDIES — CAL POLY SLO

To sit the Final Exam, each enrolled student must have completed the prerequisite coursework below. Eight bachelor parties. Eight years of applied research. Every subject tested, every skill stress-tested in the field. The following courses are on record. Transcripts are available upon request. Some of you barely passed. You know who you are.
SURV 101
Survival: Fundamentals
Introduction to keeping yourself and a group of grown men alive across a long weekend. Topics: hydration theory, navigating an unfamiliar city with 12 people and no plan, when to eat before you think you need to, basic threat assessment. The foundation everything else builds on.
Passed
BEER 101
Beer Consumption: Applied Theory
Pacing. Sequencing. The exact moment a round becomes a mistake. Managing group energy across a long day without anyone peaking too early or going full nuclear before dinner. Prerequisite for everything else on this list. This group has logged more field hours in this subject than any other. Graded Pass/Fail only. We are not here to rank anyone.
Passed
WINE 301
Wine Tasting: Intermediate
Pretending to know what you’re talking about at a tasting room while genuinely enjoying the wine. Swirling with confidence. Accepting a pour of something you’ve never heard of and saying “notes of oak” convincingly. Pre-req for Saturday afternoon in SLO wine country, where the terroir is excellent and the pretense is optional.
Passed
GAME 310
Drinking Games: Theory and Practice
A survey of competitive hydration formats. Beer pong, flip cup, kings, dice, games invented at 11pm with no rule consistency. Special emphasis on dispute resolution, table etiquette, and understanding when the game has technically been won but nobody is willing to admit it. Note: skills have atrophied significantly since initial coursework. This group has not been studying. Expect rust. Expect arguments about rules nobody fully remembers. That is also part of the curriculum.
Passed
ABNB 315
Dealing with Unstable Airbnb Hosts
Crisis management. De-escalation. Knowing the difference between “strict house rules” and “this person is going to call the police.” Reading the listing photos for red flags you only recognize in retrospect. Navigating a host who texts you 14 times and shows up unannounced. Field-tested. Unfortunately.
Passed
GOLF 520
Golf: The Gentleman’s Liability (Post-Graduate Elective)
Not an undergrad course. Most of this group didn’t pick up a club until well after graduation — a post-Cal Poly development, acquired alongside mortgages and standing tee times. How to play 18 holes while mildly to moderately impaired without injuring a crewmate or being removed from the property. Pace of play. Respecting the cart path. Accepting a double bogey without commentary. The gap between your handicap and your actual score.
Passed
CONN 401
Reading the Room: Is Conner Blacked Out?
Advanced semiotics. Behavioral pattern recognition under field conditions. Distinguishing between Conner at a 7, Conner at a 9, and Conner who has technically left the building without physically departing. Key indicators: eye contact quality, response latency, whether he is telling a story he already told or a new one. This course has no textbook. It is entirely observational.
Passed
YACK 210
Tactical Yacking: Strategic Application
Not failure. Maintenance. The tactical yack is a precision instrument — executed correctly, it resets the system and extends the night by hours. Topics: timing, discretion, execution without disruption, return to baseline, the debrief. The untrained see this as a loss. The alumni of this program know it as a tool. Use it wisely. Use it quietly.
Passed

Exam Instructions

Rules & Expectations

  • 01This is a cumulative exam. Everything you learned across the prior eight parties applies. There are no elective questions. All subjects are tested.
  • 02The fishing trip is the final practical lab. Prerequisites: none. Most of you have never fished. That is the point. Cal Poly did not prepare you for saltwater rockfishing. Cal Poly prepared you to show up anyway. Show up at the dock at 7 AM with no excuses and open hands.
  • 03CONN 401 protocols are in effect for the duration of the weekend. Watch your classmate. You know the indicators. Act accordingly.
  • 04The tactical yack, if necessary, should be executed per the YACK 210 curriculum: discrete, efficient, full system reset. Return to the group within a reasonable timeframe. No commentary from the peanut gallery. That’s what the debrief is for.
  • 05ABNB 315 protocols are standing. Read the host. Read the listing. Keep the volume reasonable between 10pm and 7am. Do not give them a reason.
  • 06We are back on the campus where this started. Walk through it like you belong — because for four years, you did. Notice it.
  • 07At some point this weekend, say something real to Matt. This accounts for the majority of your final grade. He will deflect. Say it anyway. Cal Poly taught you to do the hard thing. Do it.

Why We’re Here

To Matt

Eight parties. Eight subjects. Eight times this group showed up somewhere it had no real reason to be except that one of its members was getting married, and that was reason enough. The methodology has been consistent. The crew has been consistent. The data is clear.

Matt is the ninth. We’re back in SLO — back where it actually started, on the campus that gave all of us the same bad advice (“learn by doing”) and somehow it worked — to close out the final chapter of this particular experiment.

Matt is a man of vices, and he has never once been embarrassed about any of them. He loves sweets, wine, shopping, cooking elaborate meals, sending Instagram reels that do not land, and beautiful women — all with equal and unironic enthusiasm. He will run a statistical analysis on optimal golf swing speed, cite a peer-reviewed study, and then drink four IPAs and shoot an 18 on the back nine. Both things are true. Both are very Matt. He is simultaneously the most analytically rigorous and the most hedonistically impulsive person in any room. Wendy knows this. She said yes anyway.

He is always game. Always. Fishing, backpacking, bike camping, a hike that turns into a multi-day thing, a trip with the boys that starts as a group chat message and ends with everyone on a plane — Matt has never once said no to an adventure. If you say “I have an idea,” he has already started packing. This weekend is no different. The man is here, he is ready, and the ocean is about to meet the most enthusiastically underprepared fisherman it has ever seen.

He’s about to graduate into the next chapter. And he’s doing it with the same group he started with, back on the same coast, doing things most of us have never done and will almost certainly do badly. That feels exactly right. That’s the whole curriculum.

To Matt. The final subject. The only data point that matters.
